## Open Exploration requirements

The handbook defines three core constraints:

1. **Real problem:** the problem must genuinely exist and lack a generally
   accepted answer or adequate structure, supported by literature, data, expert
   experience, or evidence from real systems.
2. **Exploration environment:** specify what the agent observes, may change,
   and receives as feedback.
3. **Discovery signal:** state before exploration what counts as a signal,
   including a positive result, anomaly, counterexample, stable negative result,
   failure mode, or justified problem revision.

Projects must address a genuine research question rather than only business
optimization or application integration.

### Stage deliverables

- **Preliminary:** a problem-definition document of no more than four pages,
  explaining the problem, why AI is appropriate, the environment, the discovery
  signal, and the minimum baseline.
- **Second round:** a minimal runnable exploration environment, at least one
  complete execution log, baseline designs such as random or trivial systems,
  a README, and reproduction instructions.
- **Final:** an exploration report, defense materials, final code repository,
  live demonstration, and an explanation of what positive, negative, anomalous,
  or problem-revision signals mean.

### Open Exploration judging weights

- **45%** problem definition and environment-design quality;
- **35%** exploration process and scientific/research signal;
- **20%** verifiability and extensibility.

Negative results are explicitly permitted, but the process and resulting insight
must be explainable.

## Open-source, dependency, IP, and data terms

- Code is not mandatory in the preliminary round, but the open-source plan and
  release boundaries must be stated. Runnable code or equivalent verifiable
  material plus an accessible repository is required after advancement.
- From the second round onward, provide an execution entry point, installation
  and configuration instructions, random seeds, and reproduction documentation.
- Commercial APIs and closed-source models are permitted only with disclosure
  of usage, costs, access scope, alternatives/substitutability, migration cost,
  and reproducibility impact.
- Building on an existing project is permitted if the original source, the
  entrant's contribution, new innovations, and license compatibility are clear.
- All data sources, licenses, open-source dependencies, commercial services,
  external data, and key versions must be disclosed.
- Official non-public data, if any is later provided, is competition-only,
  non-redistributable, and subject to a data-use agreement and deletion/return
  requirements.
- Under the participant agreement, entrants retain their lawful project IP.
  The organizer receives limited rights to use submitted/public materials for
  judging, event display, promotion, reporting, and related competition
  communication. Necessary organizers, judges, mentors, service providers, and
  partners may access non-public material for competition purposes.
- The participant agreement applies mainland Chinese law and identifies courts
  in Hangzhou's Xihu District for unresolved disputes.
